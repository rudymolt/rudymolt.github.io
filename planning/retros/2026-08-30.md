## 1. What worked

- The Paper/HTML source boundary was explicit: Paper controlled presentation while twelve canonical HTML digests protected editorial copy. The final audit passed all twelve digests.
- Shared CSS and progressive JavaScript remained the high-leverage seams. No framework, build system, runtime, or dependency was introduced.
- Fresh-context browser review found real defects before or between handoffs, and each remediation was rebound to an exact candidate commit.
- The Pages deployment for merge commit `0db3b62a15417e4ba22dde00ad8cb65f53a53f1f` succeeded; a production Chrome canary then passed Hub, Drive Agent, and Theory at 390px, 768px, and 1440px.

## 2. What didn't

- The first broad visual pass missed native-control foreground inheritance, an inert desktop/tablet guide disclosure, cross-page list-margin leakage into the shared rail, and a footer positioned outside the document grid.
- The common cause was incomplete computed-style coverage at shared-component boundaries. Full-page screenshots alone did not reliably expose subtle cascade and containment drift.
- This was primarily **Blind** coverage at CSS seams, with a small **Tangled** cascade risk from page-local selectors. Independent verification prevented it from becoming Nodding.

## 3. What we learned

- Shared navigation and footer primitives need explicit reset and containment assertions against representative pages with hostile page-local CSS. This is now captured in `tools/audit_playbook.py` and the production contracts.
- Responsive controls should be checked for both behaviour and absence outside their active breakpoint, including the accessibility tree.
- The Paper/HTML source split is effective when visual and canonical-copy evidence remain independently durable; that vocabulary already lives in the project design artefacts and `CONTEXT.md`.
