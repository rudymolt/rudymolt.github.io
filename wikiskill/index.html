01 / The missing memory

Experience is useful only if the next attempt can build on it.

The problem WikiSkill is trying to solve

Scattered history →
compounding knowledge

Agents can already practise tasks, inspect their mistakes and rewrite their own skills. But the lessons behind those edits are often scattered across old traces and proposal histories.

WikiSkill inserts a durable knowledge layer between experience and instructions. That layer keeps the reasoning behind successful and failed edits, so future skill changes do not have to rediscover the same lessons.

05 / Transfer caveat

A good procedure can travel. A workaround can backfire.

Skill discovery and skill execution are different abilities

Promising

A spreadsheet skill evolved by Qwen-3.6-27B lifted Qwen-3.5-9B from 24.3 to 50.5, beating the 33.6 score produced by its own evolved skill.

Not automatic

A lower-level Qwen-3.5-4B spreadsheet workaround cut Gemini-3.5-Flash from 50.5 to 18.1. Transfer depends on what the skill encodes and what the receiving model can follow.

06 / What remains open

This is evidence for better skill evolution, not proof of autonomous self-improvement.

What the experiment did not test

  • 01Retrieval at scale. Active skills were inserted directly into the prompt, so finding and triggering the right skill was not tested.
  • 02Neutral stepping stones. Strict gating rejected any edit that did not immediately improve validation performance.
  • 03Forgetting. The wiki keeps growing and has no automated pruning mechanism.
  • 04Very long work. The study did not cover tasks lasting hundreds of actions or multiple hours.
  • 05Production reliability. These are controlled benchmark results from an arXiv v1 preprint, not a deployment study.
